Cover letter - submission to the Journal of Applied Physics

Dear Editor,

We submit our manuscript "Oxidation-budgeted design of lead-free RbGeI3
solar cells: back-surface-field enhancement, realistic loss budgeting and
SHAP sensitivity from SCAPS-1D optimization to manufacturing tolerances,
benchmarked against CsGeI3" for consideration as an Article in the Journal
of Applied Physics.

Lead-halide perovskites dominate single-junction efficiencies but carry a
lead-toxicity liability. Using SCAPS-1D on one frozen parameter deck, we
optimize a lead-free FTO/TiO2/RbGeI3/CuI/Au cell to 27.06% PCE and go
beyond standard parameter sweeps in ways that matter to device physics and
to fabrication:

1. A Cu2O-like back-surface field raises the ideal point to 28.01% and
   recovers about one percentage point on the identical full-realism stack
   (20.78% -> 21.76%); a block-level attribution plus a p+ control isolates
   the offset share of the gain from the trap-immunity share.
2. Ge2+ oxidation - the central liability of this absorber - is converted
   from a qualitative stability worry into a quantitative processing spec
   by mapping Ge-vacancy formation-energy thermodynamics onto the defect
   ladder (E^f >~ 0.46 eV at 300 K equilibrium for the headline efficiency;
   0.57-0.65 eV for anneal freeze-in; ambient exposure falls below 20.76%).
   The energetics are CsGeI3 DFT transferred to RbGeI3 by framework
   analogy, and the equilibrium values are lower bounds.
3. A single stacked optical/defect/parasitic loss waterfall bounds the
   experimentally plausible window instead of reporting an ideal number
   alone, and a held-out XGBoost/SHAP analysis gives design rules (bulk
   defect density, bandgap, electron mobility) that one-at-a-time sweeps
   cannot provide.
4. The solver pipeline is calibration-checked against two experimental
   beta-CsPbI3 devices reproduced within 2% in a refit - a check on the
   solver, not on this device - and the same-stack CsGeI3 gap control
   reproduces RbGeI3 exactly at Eg = 1.4 eV, isolating a band-gap effect
   at fixed electron affinity rather than A-site chemistry.

The work sits within the Journal of Applied Physics coverage of
photovoltaics, physics of devices and sensors, physics of semiconductors,
and thin films and interfaces. It is a device-physics study rather than a
routine optimization exercise: the recombination diagnostic, the interface
attribution of the back-surface-field gain, and the thermodynamic-to-process
translation are the substance of the paper, and the idealized limits are
disclosed explicitly rather than claimed as device performance. The
analysis extends the Shockley-Queisser detailed-balance framework of
Shockley and Queisser (J. Appl. Phys. 32, 510 (1961)) and the limiting
efficiency analysis of Henry (J. Appl. Phys. 51, 4494 (1980)), both in this
Journal, to a lead-free absorber with real loss channels; we also place the
stacked realistic window against the frozen inorganic perovskite database
as context.

All simulations are documented for reproducibility: Supplementary Sections
S1-S7 give the per-parameter sweep traces, the setup and provenance tables,
the loss-waterfall and oxidation-budget derivations, and a reproducibility
appendix naming the decks, run scripts, and archived result files, which are
available from the corresponding author on request. Every performance claim
sits below its Shockley-Queisser limit with the approximations disclosed in
the text.

The manuscript is original and is not under consideration elsewhere, and it
has not been published previously. All authors approve submission. The
authors have no conflicts of interest to declare. Reviewer suggestions are
welcome; suitable expertise would be lead-free perovskite simulation,
SCAPS-1D/drift-diffusion device modelling, and perovskite defect
thermodynamics.
