Cover letter — submission to Thin Solid Films (Elsevier)

Dear Editor,

We submit our manuscript "Realistic Loss Budgeting and Defect Tolerance
in Lead-Free RbGeI3 Thin-Film Solar Cells: A SCAPS-1D and SHAP Sensitivity
Analysis" for consideration as a Research Article in Thin Solid Films.

Using SCAPS-1D, we optimize a lead-free all-inorganic
FTO/TiO2 (10 nm)/RbGeI3 (700 nm)/CuI (100 nm)/Au cell and go beyond
standard parameter sweeps. A Cu2O-like p+ back-surface field raises the
stack from 26.81% (VOC 1.13 V, JSC 30.32 mA/cm2, FF 78.26%) to 30.35%
(1.18 V, 30.32 mA/cm2, 84.89%), with BSF doping — not thickness —
controlling the gain. A complete loss waterfall bounds the experimentally
plausible window: 10% front reflection costs 2.71 pp, realistic bulk
defects (1e15 cm-3) a further 1.74 pp and interface defects a further
1.54 pp, leaving 20.80% without BSF and 23.29% with BSF on the identical
defect stack (+2.49 pp recovery). Unlike standard simulation studies that
report idealized limits, this work provides a rigorous loss budget that
translates idealized SCAPS outputs into a realistic 23.3% efficiency
window bounded by actual thin-film optical reflection and native defect
densities. Junction analysis anchored to the
converged working point gives Vbi 1.46 V, depletion width 171 nm, SRH
lifetime 1000 ns and diffusion length 8.6 um, with a Mott-Schottky
baseline for experimental comparison. A SHAP sensitivity analysis over an
XGBoost surrogate of the simulation campaign independently recovers
bandgap and interface defects as the dominant design levers. The work gives experimentalists
quantitative targets (BSF NA ~1e19 cm-3, bulk Nt <= 1e15 cm-3,
Rs <= 8 ohm.cm2, mobility >= 15 cm2/Vs) rather than an idealized
efficiency number.

The work is original, not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors
approve submission.
